About

I am an Assistant Professor in the Department of Information Science at Cornell University’s Ann S. Bowers College of Computing and Information Science. My research examines how digital technologies reshape markets, organizations, competition, and consumer behavior. I use large-scale empirical analysis, field experiments, and new data infrastructures to connect information systems with their economic, organizational, and social consequences.

My work is organized into two streams. The first studies online platforms, privacy regulation, and digital markets: how firms and users respond when access to tracking data is restricted, how platform design shapes the way value is created and distributed, and how consumer behavior unfolds across online environments. This work includes the Internet Behavior Experiment, a research infrastructure for longitudinal field experiments that lets us randomly vary participants’ exposure to tracking and advertising across websites and devices and collect detailed data on each participant’s online behavior over time. The second stream studies AI adoption, human judgment, and the transformation of work: how organizations incorporate AI and automation into existing practices, and how their use reshapes tasks, responsibility, and organizational outcomes.

My research has appeared in Management Science, the ILR Review, the Yale Journal of Law & Technology, and PACM-CSCW, and has been supported by the National Science Foundation, the Mellon Foundation’s Just Futures Initiative, and Cornell’s AI Initiative. It has also informed regulatory and policy discussions through engagement with the Federal Trade Commission and the OECD.
