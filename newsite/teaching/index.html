Cornell University

Privacy and Security in the Data Economy

Fall 2020, 2021, 2022, 2024, 2026 · Upper undergraduate / graduate seminar

Show more

The course is structured around three broad topics. We first study how privacy has been understood over time, and how the economic analysis of privacy has evolved as the use of computing and connectivity has increased. We next turn to the behavioral economics of privacy, to incorporate how human behavior influences the analysis of privacy. Finally, we analyze how different economic theories can explain seemingly counter-intuitive patterns in information security.

Networks II: Market Design

Spring 2020–2025 · Upper undergraduate / graduate

Show more

This course covers mathematical models to understand how markets operate and how we can design them to influence agents' behavior and produce the outcomes we need. We also explore what defines network economies, multi-sided markets, and platforms.

Digital Markets and Online Platforms

Fall 2023 · Ph.D. seminar

Show more

This doctoral seminar examines how digital transformation reshapes markets, platforms, and economic behavior through empirical and experimental approaches. Students engage with research across disciplines to understand the economics of digitization and develop an original research project on a related topic.

Research, Innovation, and Entrepreneurship Challenge Lab

Spring 2015, Fall 2016 · Engineering

Show more

I co-developed this class with faculty at P. Universidad Católica de Chile and UC Berkeley's Sutardja Center for Entrepreneurship and Technology. Students develop a technology-based entrepreneurial idea and produce a prototype and pitch for their project. The course ends in a competition where winners receive seed funding for their projects (~US$7,500 per project). In my two semesters teaching this course, several teams of my students won the competition.
